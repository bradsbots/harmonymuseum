The largest room · 1805

The Harmonist Barn

Built by the Harmony Society for sheep, raised by the Mennonites for hay, and now the best-lit barn in Butler County.

Four bays of hand-hewn timber, 42 by 64 feet, with room for a ceremony at one end and dancing at the other. The lights stay up. Rentals run Friday to Monday, so you decorate on Friday, celebrate on Saturday and clean up on Sunday.

Hall & wine cellar · 1809

Stewart Hall and the wine cellar

The practical room — kitchen, bar, stage, accessible restrooms — with the Harmony Society's stone wine cellar directly below.

The hall seats 120 at tables, with a raised stage-and-bar area for a band or a head table. Down a stone stair is the vaulted cellar the Harmonists dug in 1809: 42 by 18 feet of flagstone and candlelight, for a toast, a tasting or the photographs.

Ceremonies & concerts · 1825

The Mennonite Meetinghouse

A small, reverent room on the National Register, kept exactly as the Mennonite community built it.

Original bleacher-style benches for 130 to 150, a pulpit platform for the ceremony, and acoustics that flatter a string quartet or a single voice. Bring your own restroom facility; the museum will tell you who to call.

How renting works

Four things to know before you ask

1

A security deposit is required

Equal to the rental fee, charged when the contract is signed, and returned after the event if the room is clean, nothing is damaged and the key comes back.

2

An insurance form

Every rental needs an event-insurance form on file. Most homeowner's policies or a one-day event policy will do; the office will send you the form.

3

Members pay less

Museum members get discounted rental rates on all three venues. Membership starts at $25.

4

A person replies

Requests go to the museum office, staffed by volunteers. Expect an answer within three business days; call (724) 452-7341 if it's urgent.
