## 1. Harmony Museum
- Listing: https://harmonymuseum.org/places/harmony-museum/ (lastmod 2025-06-10)
- Address (listing): 218 Mercer Street, Harmony, Pennsylvania 16037
- Address (/museum-properties/): **218 Mercer St.** (listed there as "Harmony Museum & General Store")

Originally the Harmony Society warehouse with a well preserved wine cellar, this magnificent building serves as the main Harmony Museum. It was built in 1809 from locally made bricks and was expanded several times over the last 200+ years. The Harmony Museum hosts the rich local history that includes presentations of George Washington's 1753 mission; Harmonists; Mennonites; the Ball Collection of outstanding sporting rifles by 19th century Harmony gunsmith Charles Flowers; the Harmony Line: PHB & NC Railway and past history and memorabilia from local, "Doc Stewart."

Image: https://harmonymuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Properties-Museum-1-768x607.jpg

## 2. Wagner/Bentle Haus
- Listing: https://harmonymuseum.org/places/wagner-bentle-haus/ (lastmod 2025-11-20)
- Address (listing): 222 Mercer Street, Butler County, Pennsylvania 16037
- Address (/museum-properties/): **222 Mercer St.** (spelled "Wagner/Bentel Haus" there; /history-of-harmony/ dates it 1812 and calls it "home of the museum shop"; /plan-a-visit/ says the Museum Shop is "across the parking lot from the museum, at 222 Mercer St.")

This duplex was built in the early 1800s for two sisters who were members of the Harmony Society, and their families. Among the interesting construction details that can be seen inside are back-to-back stone fireplaces and cooking hearths. The building is part of the museum tour.

Image: https://harmonymuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Properties-Wagner-1-768x576.jpg

## 3. Ziegler Log House
- Listing: https://harmonymuseum.org/places/ziegler-log-house/ (lastmod 2025-11-20)
- Address (listing): 538 Main Street, Butler County, Pennsylvania 16037
- Address (/museum-properties/): **546 Main St.** — CONFLICT: the two pages disagree (538 vs 546). /museum-properties/ also words the description "to illustrate Mennonite daily life" instead of "Society members' daily life".

This reconstructed 19th century cabin with open fireplace is filled with period furnishings to illustrate Society members' daily life. Also on the tour, it is a favorite of children as they try to envision pioneer life.

## 4. Harmonist/Ziegler Barn
- Listing: https://harmonymuseum.org/places/harmonist-ziegler-barn/ (lastmod 2026-07-29)
- Address (listing): Mercer Road, Harmony, Butler County, Pennsylvania 16037
- Address (/museum-properties/): **303 Mercer Rd.** (/haymaker-ii-flea-market/ says "Harmony Barn, 303 Mercer Street, Harmony")

Built in 1805 as a somewhat lower structure for sheep, the barn was enlarged by Mennonites who bought the property when the Harmonists moved to Indiana. Mennonite farmers extended the roof upward to accommodate the mounds of loose hay needed for their animals. It retains its early 1800s rustic character and the charming interior can be seen by appointment. It is a popular location as a rental for parties, events and weddings.

Image: https://harmonymuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Properties-Harmonist-Barn-1-768x512.jpg

## 5. Mennonite Meetinghouse
- Listing: https://harmonymuseum.org/places/mennonite-meeting-house/ (lastmod 2020-11-01)
- Address (listing): Mennonite Meeting House, 114 Wise Road, Harmony, Butler County, Pennsylvania 16037
- Address (/museum-properties/): **114 Wise Rd.**

Another beautiful property kept in its original condition by Harmony Museum, this 1825 building was used as church, school and social center for the Mennonite community headed by Abraham Ziegler. Music played in the small interior resonates more beautifully than in many newer and larger churches. It is also popular for small weddings and intimate concerts.

Image: https://harmonymuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Properties-meeting-house-sm-1-768x576.jpg

## 6. Visitor Information Cabin
- Listing: https://harmonymuseum.org/places/visitor-center/ (lastmod 2020-11-01)
- Address (listing): 551 Main Street, Harmony, Butler County, Pennsylvania 16037
- /museum-properties/ calls this the **"Washington 1753 Cabin"** (no street address given there) and ends the description differently: "It was donated to the Harmony Museum by the Carothers family. Come and visit this fascinating display which contains information about George Washington's historic visit to the area, where our future president was almost assassinated!" The America 250 post (June 2026) also calls it "Washington's 1753 Cabin".

Our newest acquisition is this reconstructed cabin on Main Street that is used to post visitor information and event news for Harmony and the surrounding area. Built around 1820 by the Mennonite Ziegler family for one of their sons, it stood on one of several farms between Harmony and Evans City owned by Abraham Ziegler descendants. It was donated to Harmony Museum by the Carothers family and a new exhibit is planned for the interior in the near future.

Image: https://harmonymuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Properties-Visitors-Cabin-1-768x511.jpg

## 7. Harmonist Cemetery
- Listing: https://harmonymuseum.org/places/harmonist-cemetery/ (lastmod 2020-11-01)
- Address (listing): Intersection of Evans City Rd (Rt. 68) and Edmund St., Butler County, Pennsylvania 16037
- Address (/museum-properties/): **Intersection of Evans City Rd. (Rt. 68) and Edmund St.** — that page says "About 100 Harmonists are buried here" where the listing says "More than 100".

This may just be the most well known of Harmony Museum's properties due to its location along Route 68 where it can easily be seen by passing motorists. The stone walls still stand perfectly straight and the unique gate, shaped to represent the tablets of the Ten Commandments and weighing more than a ton, still turn easily with a push of your hand. More than 100 Harmonists are buried here, but they have no tombstones since they believed they were recognized in heaven and no longer needed recognition here on earth. There is one marker that is not a traditional tombstone but rather a commemorative stone for George Rapp's son.

Image: https://harmonymuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Harmonist-Cemetery-2-768x576.jpg

## 8. Rapp's Seat
- Listing: https://harmonymuseum.org/places/rapps-seat/ (lastmod 2021-01-30)
- Address (listing): Rapp's Seat, Rapp's Seat Trail, Harmony, Butler County, Pennsylvania 16037
- Not listed on /museum-properties/.

A short, steep climb up a nearby hillside brings the visitor to Rapp's Seat. Father Rapp, head of the Harmony Society, found this spot and realized he could watch over his "flock" of followers from its height overlooking the town and nearby fields. It must have been a wonderful place for quiet contemplation, and it still is today.

Image: https://harmonymuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Properties-Rapps-Seat-1-768x576.jpg

## 9. Weavers Cabin
- Listing: https://harmonymuseum.org/places/weavers-cabin/ (lastmod 2020-11-01)
- Address (listing): Harmony Weaver's Cabin, 245 Mercer Street, Butler County, Pennsylvania 16037
- Not listed on /museum-properties/. /plan-a-visit/ says: "Our weavers cabin is rented to a private group now. They are located in our quaint log cabin at the intersection of Mercer St. and Spring St. (almost directly across from the Harmony Inn)."

Big news: the Harmony Museum's Weavers Cabin in now rented by the volunteers. This will allow them to sell their goods, as well as continuing to demonstrate and educate visitors. This reconstructed two-story cabin houses the Harmony Museum Weavers. This talented group works on antique and reproduction looms to replicate the goods that the Harmonists produced for sale here. Quality woolen and linen fabrics and even silk came from the looms that were employed by the enterprising Harmonists. Several days a week the Museum Weavers demonstrate this art to visitors and sell their wares. Follow them on Facebook to get their updated hours of operation and all they have to offer at [Weavers Cabin of Harmony](https://www.facebook.com/Weavers-Cabin-of-Harmony-114067673625014/)

Image: https://harmonymuseum.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/07/Properties-Weavers-Cabin-1-768x576.jpg
